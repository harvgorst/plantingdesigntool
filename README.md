# SevenLayers

SevenLayers is a planting-only design tool for garden designers. Import an existing site plan, place plants over it at true scale, study sun and shade, and get a costed plant schedule, supplier order lists, an interest calendar, a client booklet and a scaled plan sheet.

SevenLayers is a single HTML file. Download `index.html` and open it in a browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari); there is nothing to install and no account. It can also be served from any static web host.

## What it does

- **Plan**: import a site plan (SVG, PDF page 1, PNG or JPEG) as an underlay and set its scale from two points a known distance apart. Place single plants or groups, or a row of evenly spaced plants between two points (for hedges and edging); with the Row tool, drag the dot in the middle of a row to bend it into a curve, and its plants re-space evenly along it. Existing plants are drawn dashed, with Retain / Prune / Remove status, measured size and notes per plant. an As planted button to show plants at pot size instead of mature spread, a Layers menu to show any combination of the seven layers, and a Shift-drag selection box.
- **Structure, planting and bulbs in one plan**: trees, shrubs, climbers and existing plants go exactly where you click, drawn at their own size in their own species colour and pattern, just like the perennials, keeping a centre dot (existing ones have a dashed edge). Perennials and ground cover are painted onto a 30 cm interlocked grid (the spacing can be changed) and drawn at their planting spacing, so neighbours touch, each species in its own colour and pattern; drag to paint, Alt-drag to rub out, Shift to place one off the grid. Painting is for the beds: once a bed is drawn, perennials and bulbs are painted only inside beds, and outside them each click places one plant just where you click, so bulbs can go in a lawn or meadow and a tree in open ground. Bulbs are a layer of their own over the top, on a finer grid (15 cm by default) among the perennials, so they can be planned after the planting; while you place bulbs the rest of the plan fades back. Touching plants of one species are labelled once as a block, and a row once at its middle with the count for the whole row. Labels sit in a column down each side of the plan, one per species on that side with its total, joined to the plants by fine metro-map lines that run level, at 45° and upright (Preferences → Label placement puts them back on the plan instead, where they never overlap and can be dragged). If a plan sheet has no room for the columns at the scale you chose, its labels go on the plan rather than the scale changing. The As planted button shows trees and shrubs at their pot size instead of their mature spread.
- **Oudolf drawing, set for each bed** (Drawing in the Area tool bar for the selected bed, or the Oudolf button on the toolbar and the O key, which switch the selected bed, or every bed when none is selected; with no beds drawn it applies to the whole plan; switching only changes the drawing and how the planting is counted, never the plants): new beds start with tight circles, and in a bed set to Oudolf the perennials and ground cover are drawn as areas that share their borders, in the manner of Piet Oudolf's and Henk Gerritsen's plans, keeping each plant's colour and pattern. You still paint them onto the grid; painting over a neighbour moves the border. Each area is labelled with its count, worked out from its size and the plant's number per square metre, which is set in the bar under the toolbar when you pick the plant or click its area, and kept with the species. Click an area to select all of it and drag to move it.
- **Areas** (the Area tool, A or B): each area is a border bed, a guild, existing woodland, a hedge (with its height and whether it is evergreen or deciduous; it casts shade in the Sun view, lighter when a deciduous hedge is bare), a building (with its height; it casts shade in the Sun view), a path or hard surface (paving, gravel, bark, grass or decking) or water (pond, lake, river, stream or rill), each drawn with its own texture and named in its middle. Plants can go on any of them (a green roof, a pond's aquatics and marginals, bulbs in a lawn): areas describe the site and never stop you placing a plant. Any area can have straight edges instead of curves (buildings and hedges start straight). **Beds**: draw a bed's outline with the Area tool, clicking round its edge and then on the first point again; the edge is drawn as a smooth curve through the points. Inside a bed the planting grid is centred on it, so the rows sit evenly from edge to edge, painting stays inside it, and in a bed drawn the Oudolf way the areas run right out to the bed's edge and stop there. Each bed keeps its own grid, so reshaping it never shifts the planting; moving the whole bed takes its planting with it. Bed points snap to 2 cm, or onto another bed's point when close, so beds can share an edge. Beds can only be selected, moved, reshaped (drag a point; drag the small dot between two points to add one; double-click a point to remove it) or removed with the Bed tool, so they can't be knocked by accident. A selected bed can be given a name, shown in capitals just above it on the plan and in the booklet, and a colour that tints its ground (No colour puts it back).
- **Guilds**: set a bed's Type to Guild for a forest-garden group, such as an apple with its comfrey, chives and currants. It works like any bed on the plan, and is named after its tallest plant ("Apple guild") unless you give it a name. The plant schedule lists each guild with its plants from the tallest layer down, and the presentation has a Guilds page (it can be switched off with the other pages).
- **Existing woodland**: set a bed's Type to Existing woodland and give its canopy height, how dense the canopy is (dense, dappled or open) and whether it is deciduous or evergreen. It is drawn once, with a gently scalloped crown edge and its name and canopy in the middle, instead of being filled with trees. In the Sun view its canopy casts shade like a building of that height, lighter for dappled or open woodland and for deciduous woodland out of leaf (November to mid-April), and the hours-of-sun map counts that. It is listed with the existing plants, with its area, and never counted as plants to buy. Shade plants can be painted beneath it as in any bed.
- **Editing**: undo and redo (including species edits), duplicate (Ctrl+D), copy and paste, arrow-key nudging, a measure tool, and labels that can be dragged clear of a crowded spot with a leader line back to the plant. When a plant is picked or selected, the bar under the toolbar shows its plan colour and pattern next to its name, with an Automatic button to put them back. It also shows spread and height for trees and shrubs, and spacing for bulbs. Changes there apply to the whole species and can be undone.
- **Sun study**: enter the site's latitude and longitude, pick a date and time, and see the shadows cast by plants (from their height) and by buildings, walls and fences drawn for the study. Play the day as an animation, or switch to a map of hours of direct sun (full shade to full sun).
- **Season view**: colour the plan by what is in flower or of interest in a chosen month.
- **Plant schedule**: new plants with layer, supply, supplier, quantity and cost, plus a separate table of existing plants. Copies straight into a spreadsheet.
- **Order lists**: one list per supplier, with codes and prices, flagging lines where you need more than the stock on the supplier's availability list.
- **Interest calendar**: months across, species down. Each plant is either Flowers or a Crop (chosen in the species form under In season), with one set of months and one colour: when it flowers, or for a crop when it is harvested. Both show the same way, as one bar in their colour, on the calendar, in the booklet and in the season view on the plan; crops are marked Crop. The row underneath shows the colour of each month and how many plants are in season, and a month with nothing in season is pointed out.
- **Plant database**: every species entered, shared across all gardens. Searchable, filterable by layer, sortable. **Look up from Plants For A Future**: start typing a botanical (or common) name in the species form and matches from a built-in list of about 8,800 useful plants appear; picking one fills in the common name, layer, height, spread, flowering months and foliage, but never anything you've typed yourself, and a cultivar keeps its own name and takes its species' details. Mark a plant as a Crop and its months become the harvest, taken from when its fruit or seed ripens (only for plants grown for fruit, nuts or seed; leafy crops are left for you to set). **Add a list** on the Plant database page takes many botanical names at once, pasted one per line: each new species is created and filled in where Plants For A Future lists it, names already in your database are skipped, and any it can't find are listed for you to fill in. Flower colour always stays your choice. The species form asks for six things up front (botanical name, common name, layer, Flowers or Crop with months and colour, height and spread, and a photo); cultivar, foliage, spread as planted, spacing, plan colour, supply, cost, supplier and whether it is an existing plant are under More details.
- **Supplier availability**: import a nursery's availability list (Excel, CSV or pasted rows), kept separate from your plants. Tick lines to add them to your plant database with price, supplier, pot size and code; matching plants have their price and supplier updated.
- **Presentation**: a client booklet (A4 landscape PDF) with a cover and your logo, the planting plan numbered to the palette, a numbered plant palette of close-up photos, the plant schedule, the interest calendar and a pricing page with your own lines and optional VAT. The plan is drawn as vector lines and the text can be searched and copied. The booklet PDF is drawn directly: real, selectable text (in Times, which every PDF reader has), coloured blocks and rules as shapes, the plan as vector lines, and photos cropped to fill their frame; it takes well under a second for a few pages.
- **Scaled plan sheet**: the plan on its own at a true scale (1:20 to 1:500) on A4 to A1, with a title block and plant key.
- **Aerial photo under the plan**: Site plan → Aerial photo from map (or Garden details → Find the garden on the map) opens a map with Esri World Imagery. Search an address or postcode (OpenStreetMap's free address search) or type latitude, longitude, then pan and zoom until the garden sits in the white frame, which is the plan at its real size. The photo then sits under the plan at true scale, turned with north, trimmed to the plot, with its own transparency, and the sun study location is filled in. Only the garden's position is kept; the photo is fetched fresh from Esri with your own free ArcGIS Location Platform API key (Preferences → Aerial photos) whenever it's shown or exported, always credited "Powered by Esri · Source: Esri, Vantor, Earthstar Geographics, and the GIS User Community". The key stays on your computer and is left out of backups and PDFs.
- **A plant library for each garden**: the list beside the plan is that garden's own selection; a new garden starts empty. Add plants picks species from your plant database (with search) or makes a new one, which joins both. The Plant database page can also add ticked species to the open garden. A species leaves a garden's library from its edit panel once it's off the plan, and stays in the database. Plants recorded as existing on a site stay with that garden and aren't offered to others. Gardens made before this start with the species already on their plan.
- **Plant library filters**: chips under the search box show just the layers you pick (any mix), or only the species used in this garden; the full list is grouped under layer headings. The choice is remembered.
- **Plant photos from Wikimedia Commons**: Find photo on a palette card (or Find a photo online in the species form) searches Wikimedia Commons by botanical name, cultivar first and then the species, clearly labelled, and offers only photos whose licence allows use in client work (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA). The one you click is copied into your own storage with its photographer and licence, and the booklet prints the credit on the photo. Find missing photos online steps through every plant still without one. Needs an internet connection.
- **Backup**: download a garden, the plant library or everything as one file, and restore from it.
- **Preferences**: a theme (Eiderdown, Woodwork, 98 or root), label size and text, grid, rulers, snap spacing (5 cm–1 m), default view, currency, and colours for each layer.

Plants are grouped by the seven-layer system: Canopy, Understory, Shrub, Herbaceous, Ground cover, Tubers & bulbs, Climbers.

## Files

- `index.html`: the whole app (HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file).
- `example/example-site-plan.svg`: a made-up site plan used by the tests.
- `tests/`: automated checks that open the app in a headless browser.

## Storage

Everything is saved in the browser you use, in its IndexedDB storage: gardens, the plant database, supplier availability lists, preferences, imported plans, plant photos and the logo. Nothing is sent to a server. PDFs and backups are ordinary file downloads. Two tabs open on the same copy stay in step.

Because the data lives in that browser, it stays on that computer and in that browser only; clearing the browser's site data deletes it. Use **Preferences → Backup → Download everything** regularly, and to move your work to another computer or browser, then **Restore from a backup** there. Opened from disk (`file://`), the data belongs to that file's location, so keep `index.html` in one place.

Fonts load from Google Fonts and the PDF, spreadsheet and drawing libraries from cdnjs and jsDelivr, so making PDFs and importing spreadsheets need an internet connection. Everything else works offline.

## Tests

The tests need Node.js and Playwright.

```bash
npm install
```

```bash
npx playwright install chromium
```

```bash
npm test
```

To run only some tests, give part of a test's name:

```bash
node tests/smoke.js sun
```

Most tests keep their data in memory (`tests/mock-runtime.js`), so they never touch saved gardens; one runs against the browser's real storage and checks a garden survives a reload. The PDF tests load jsPDF and svg2pdf.js from their CDNs; to run offline, set `SL_LIBS` to a folder holding `jspdf.umd.min.js`, `svg2pdf.umd.min.js`, `xlsx.full.min.js`, `leaflet.js` and `leaflet.css`. A few checks read the PDFs back with `pdftotext` and `pdfimages` (from poppler) and are skipped when they aren't installed.

## Plant data

The built-in plant list comes from [Plants For A Future](https://pfaf.org) (pfaf.org), whose plant information is licensed under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). It was taken from the [pfaf-data](https://github.com/saulshanabrook/pfaf-data) snapshot of 17 October 2026 and trimmed to the fields SevenLayers uses by `tools/pfaf_extract.py` (`python3 tools/pfaf_extract.py data.sqlite pfaf.txt`). Layers are worked out from PFAF's habit (trees of 10 m or more are canopy, smaller ones understory; low perennials it lists as ground cover are ground cover), and spreads PFAF doesn't give are estimated for trees and shrubs from their height. PFAF's photos are under a different licence and are not used.
